Richardson Extrapolation

Numerical Differentiation Enhancement Technique

Mathematical Discussion

Introduction

Richardson extrapolation is a powerful technique used to improve the accuracy of numerical approximations by combining multiple estimates obtained with different step sizes. This method is particularly useful in numerical differentiation and integration.

Theoretical Foundation

Consider a numerical approximation \( D(h) \) that depends on a step size \( h \). If the approximation has an error expansion of the form:

\[ D(h) = D^* + c_1 h^{p} + c_2 h^{p+1} + c_3 h^{p+2} + \cdots \]

where \( D^* \) is the exact value, \( p \) is the order of the method, and \( c_1, c_2, c_3, \ldots \) are constants independent of \( h \).

Central Difference Formula

For numerical differentiation, we use the central difference formula as our base method:

\[ f'(x) \approx D(h) = \frac{f(x+h) - f(x-h)}{2h} \]

The error expansion for central difference is:

\[ D(h) = f'(x) + c_1 h^2 + c_2 h^4 + c_3 h^6 + \cdots \]

Note that only even powers of \( h \) appear due to the symmetry of the central difference formula.

Richardson Extrapolation Formula

Given two approximations \( D(h) \) and \( D(h/2) \), we can eliminate the leading error term:

\[ D_1(h) = \frac{4 D(h/2) - D(h)}{4 - 1} = \frac{4 D(h/2) - D(h)}{3} \]

This new approximation \( D_1(h) \) has an error of order \( O(h^4) \) instead of \( O(h^2) \).

The general Richardson extrapolation formula for level \( j \) is:

\[ D_j(h) = \frac{4^j D_{j-1}(h/2) - D_{j-1}(h)}{4^j - 1} \]

where \( D_0(h) = D(h) \) is the original central difference approximation.

Extrapolation Table

The Richardson extrapolation process creates a triangular table:

\[ \begin{array}{ccccc} D_0(h) & & & & \\ D_0(h/2) & D_1(h) & & & \\ D_0(h/4) & D_1(h/2) & D_2(h) & & \\ D_0(h/8) & D_1(h/4) & D_2(h/2) & D_3(h) & \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots \end{array} \]

The diagonal elements \( D_k(h) \) provide increasingly accurate estimates of the derivative.

Advantages

  • Improved Accuracy: Each extrapolation level increases the order of accuracy.
  • Efficiency: Reuses previously computed function evaluations.
  • Error Estimation: The difference between successive diagonal elements provides an error estimate.
  • Versatility: Applicable to various numerical methods beyond differentiation.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Derivative of \( f(x) = x^2 \sin(x) \) at \( x = 1.0 \)

Problem Statement

Find the derivative of \( f(x) = x^2 \sin(x) \) at \( x = 1.0 \) using Richardson extrapolation with initial step size \( h_0 = 0.1 \) and 4 levels of extrapolation.

Solution

Step 1: Calculate the exact derivative for comparison

Using the product rule:

\[ f'(x) = 2x \sin(x) + x^2 \cos(x) \]

At \( x = 1.0 \):

\[ f'(1.0) = 2(1)\sin(1) + (1)^2\cos(1) = 2(0.8414709848) + 0.5403023059 = 2.2232442755 \]

Step 2: Compute central difference approximations

For \( h_0 = 0.1 \):

\[ D_0(0.1) = \frac{f(1.1) - f(0.9)}{0.2} \]
\[ f(1.1) = (1.1)^2 \sin(1.1) = 1.21 \times 0.8912073601 = 1.0783609057 \]
\[ f(0.9) = (0.9)^2 \sin(0.9) = 0.81 \times 0.7833269096 = 0.6344947968 \]
\[ D_0(0.1) = \frac{1.0783609057 - 0.6344947968}{0.2} = 2.2193305445 \]

For \( h_1 = 0.05 \):

\[ D_0(0.05) = \frac{f(1.05) - f(0.95)}{0.1} \]
\[ f(1.05) = 1.1025 \times \sin(1.05) = 1.1025 \times 0.8674232256 = 0.9563341062 \]
\[ f(0.95) = 0.9025 \times \sin(0.95) = 0.9025 \times 0.8134155047 = 0.7341074930 \]
\[ D_0(0.05) = \frac{0.9563341062 - 0.7341074930}{0.1} = 2.2222661320 \]

For \( h_2 = 0.025 \):

\[ D_0(0.025) = \frac{f(1.025) - f(0.975)}{0.05} = 2.2229986459 \]

For \( h_3 = 0.0125 \):

\[ D_0(0.0125) = \frac{f(1.0125) - f(0.9875)}{0.025} = 2.2231822997 \]

Step 3: Apply Richardson extrapolation

Level 1 (j=1):

\[ D_1(0.1) = \frac{4 D_0(0.05) - D_0(0.1)}{3} = \frac{4(2.2222661320) - 2.2193305445}{3} = 2.2232446612 \]
\[ D_1(0.05) = \frac{4 D_0(0.025) - D_0(0.05)}{3} = \frac{4(2.2229986459) - 2.2222661320}{3} = 2.2232428172 \]
\[ D_1(0.025) = \frac{4 D_0(0.0125) - D_0(0.025)}{3} = \frac{4(2.2231822997) - 2.2229986459}{3} = 2.2232435176 \]

Level 2 (j=2):

\[ D_2(0.1) = \frac{16 D_1(0.05) - D_1(0.1)}{15} = \frac{16(2.2232428172) - 2.2232446612}{15} = 2.2232426945 \]
\[ D_2(0.05) = \frac{16 D_1(0.025) - D_1(0.05)}{15} = \frac{16(2.2232435176) - 2.2232428172}{15} = 2.2232435643 \]

Level 3 (j=3):

\[ D_3(0.1) = \frac{64 D_2(0.05) - D_2(0.1)}{63} = \frac{64(2.2232435643) - 2.2232426945}{63} = 2.2232435781 \]

Step 5: Error Analysis

Exact value: \( f'(1.0) = 2.2232442755 \)
Richardson estimate: \( D_3 = 2.2232435781 \)
Absolute error: \( |2.2232442755 - 2.2232435781| = 6.974 \times 10^{-7} \)
Relative error: \( 3.14 \times 10^{-5}\% \)

Compare this to the initial central difference error:
Initial error: \( |2.2232442755 - 2.2193305445| = 3.914 \times 10^{-3} \)
Improvement factor: approximately 5600×!

Example 2: Derivative of \( f(x) = e^x \cos(x) \) at \( x = 0.5 \)

Problem Statement

Find the derivative of \( f(x) = e^x \cos(x) \) at \( x = 0.5 \) using Richardson extrapolation with initial step size \( h_0 = 0.2 \) and 3 levels of extrapolation.

Solution

Step 1: Calculate the exact derivative for comparison

Using the product rule:

\[ f'(x) = e^x \cos(x) - e^x \sin(x) = e^x(\cos(x) - \sin(x)) \]

At \( x = 0.5 \):

\[ f'(0.5) = e^{0.5}(\cos(0.5) - \sin(0.5)) = 1.6487212707 \times (0.8775825619 - 0.4794255386) = 0.6565395819 \]

Step 2: Compute central difference approximations

For \( h_0 = 0.2 \):

\[ D_0(0.2) = \frac{f(0.7) - f(0.3)}{0.4} \]
\[ f(0.7) = e^{0.7} \cos(0.7) = 2.0137527075 \times 0.7648421873 = 1.5402046973 \]
\[ f(0.3) = e^{0.3} \cos(0.3) = 1.3498588076 \times 0.9553364891 = 1.2895769936 \]
\[ D_0(0.2) = \frac{1.5402046973 - 1.2895769936}{0.4} = 0.6265692593 \]

For \( h_1 = 0.1 \):

\[ D_0(0.1) = \frac{f(0.6) - f(0.4)}{0.2} \]
\[ f(0.6) = e^{0.6} \cos(0.6) = 1.8221188004 \times 0.8253356149 = 1.5038618829 \]
\[ f(0.4) = e^{0.4} \cos(0.4) = 1.4918246976 \times 0.9210609940 = 1.3740588078 \]
\[ D_0(0.1) = \frac{1.5038618829 - 1.3740588078}{0.2} = 0.6490153755 \]

For \( h_2 = 0.05 \):

\[ D_0(0.05) = \frac{f(0.55) - f(0.45)}{0.1} = 0.6546096915 \]

Step 3: Apply Richardson extrapolation

Level 1 (j=1):

\[ D_1(0.2) = \frac{4 D_0(0.1) - D_0(0.2)}{3} = \frac{4(0.6490153755) - 0.6265692593}{3} = 0.6564974142 \]
\[ D_1(0.1) = \frac{4 D_0(0.05) - D_0(0.1)}{3} = \frac{4(0.6546096915) - 0.6490153755}{3} = 0.6564744635 \]

Level 2 (j=2):

\[ D_2(0.2) = \frac{16 D_1(0.1) - D_1(0.2)}{15} = \frac{16(0.6564744635) - 0.6564974142}{15} = 0.6564729370 \]

Absolute error: \( |0.6565395819 - 0.6564729370| = 6.664 \times 10^{-5} \)
Relative error: \( 0.0102\% \)

Compare this to the initial central difference error:
Initial error: \( |0.6565395819 - 0.6265692593| = 2.997 \times 10^{-2} \)
Improvement factor: approximately 450×!
